\section*{Summary}

The work presents the design, implementation, and evaluation of an algorithmic trading system based on Machine Learning techniques for the
identification and optimization of LONG/SHORT movements applied to financial futures. The main objective is to autonomously operate a diversified
portfolio (target value of USD 1 million) across 13 futures contracts (currencies, indices, commodities, and bonds), maximizing profit while
controlling risk. The proposed architecture is hybrid: it combines supervised models (LSTM and GRU) to generate directional signals and a regression
layer to estimate returns.

The data flow follows an ETL pipeline organized in Bronze-Silver-Gold layers: ingestion of OHLCV data at one-minute frequency, cleaning and temporal
standardization to UTC, resampling to 4-hour candles, and computation of an extensive set of features (momentum, volatility, volume, structural
indicators, and features derived from swing charts). Feature selection (correlation, LASSO) is applied to reduce dimensionality and prevent data
leakage. The target label is constructed using the triple barrier method (Stop Loss, Take Profit, time expiry) and is complemented by regression of
logarithmic returns.

Models are trained with PyTorch using a walk-forward (moving window) strategy to respect temporal structure and avoid leakage. The solution comprises
three levels: L1 (detection of significant movement), L2 (BUY/SELL direction), and L3 (return regression); the final decision is obtained by
soft-voting weighted by the estimated return. RobustScaler and regularization techniques (dropout) are employed to ensure robustness against outliers
and regime changes.

Backtesting is performed on 4-hour data and includes prediction selection phases and operational simulation with different modalities (long/short,
scale-in, dynamic sizing). Evaluated metrics include Sharpe, Max Drawdown, Calmar, and CAGR; Monte Carlo simulations are also used to analyze
robustness and sensitivity. For deployment the system is containerized (Docker) and integrates monitoring with Prometheus/Loki, visualization in
Grafana, and alerts via Slack. The work includes ethical and economic analysis, training and backtest results, and proposes future lines such as
unsupervised learning, MLOps orchestration, and optimization with Pyomo.
